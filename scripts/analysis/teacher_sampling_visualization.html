FPS is video evidence density. It means observations per second of the recorded task. It does not measure API calls per second or how fast the model generates its answer. Multiple camera panels at one timestamp count as one observation time.

The last source frame is always retained. It can create a shorter final gap, so rates above describe the regular cadence. “Observations” counts actual timestamps, not images multiplied by camera count.

Why sampled playback looks slow or choppy

Playback and sampling are separate. At 1×, the clock advances at the original episode speed. A sparse stream repeats its last image until the next supplied frame, then jumps forward. Stepping to the next teacher frame jumps over the intervening video time.

The fork’s reviewer uses this hold behavior in sampled mode. Actual model requests contain the selected image sequence; they are not paced out at the displayed FPS.
